\chapter{Results \& Discussions}

Validation of the multi-artefact digitisation pipeline evaluates processing efficacy through visual output comparison, quantitative quality metrics, and system throughput benchmarks. Section 5.1 presents the simulation and visual results across distinct artefact stages, comparing raw, preprocessed, enhanced, and binarised outputs. Section 5.2 establishes the experimental evaluations using self-reference image quality metrics, verifying signal preservation and noise suppression. Section 5.3 investigates execution performance and resource requirements, contrasting local workstation benchmarks against cloud deployment footprints. Finally, Section 5.4 outlines the inferences drawn from algorithm routing strategies, establishing suitability guidelines for South Asian historical records.

\section{Experimental Results}

The experimental evaluations use self-reference quality verification because historical scans lack clean, ground-truth targets. A pseudo-reference image $I_{\text{ref}}$ is generated by applying an edge-preserving bilateral filter on the enhanced scan $I_{\text{enh}}$ as defined in Eqn. \ref{eqn:ref_img}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eqn:ref_img}
I_{\text{ref}} = \text{BilateralFilter}(I_{\text{enh}}, d=9, \sigma_C=25, \sigma_S=25)
\end{equation}
This baseline preserves stroke boundaries while smoothing out fine background noise. The mean squared error (MSE) between this pseudo-reference and the enhanced image is calculated via Eqn. \ref{eqn:mse}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eqn:mse}
\text{MSE} = \frac{1}{H \times W} \sum_{x=0}^{W-1} \sum_{y=0}^{H-1} \left( I_{\text{ref}}(x, y) - I_{\text{enh}}(x, y) \right)^2
\end{equation}
Using the derived MSE, the self-reference Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) is formulated as shown in Eqn. \ref{eqn:psnr}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eqn:psnr}
\text{PSNR} = 10 \cdot \log_{10}\left( \frac{255^2}{\text{MSE}} \right)
\end{equation}
Structural similarity (SSIM) evaluates local structure, contrast, and luminance preservation across the image coordinates, defined in Eqn. \ref{eqn:ssim}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eqn:ssim}
\text{SSIM}(x, y) = \frac{(2\mu_x\mu_y + C_1)(2\sigma_{xy} + C_2)}{(\mu_x^2 + \mu_y^2 + C_1)(\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2 + C_2)}
\end{equation}
To calculate character contrast against local backgrounds, the Contrast-to-Noise Ratio (CNR) is computed as shown in Eqn. \ref{eqn:cnr}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eqn:cnr}
\text{CNR} = \frac{|\mu_{\text{foreground}} - \mu_{\text{background}}|}{\sigma_{\text{background}}}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{\text{foreground}}$ and $\mu_{\text{background}}$ represent the mean intensity values of binarised foreground stroke and background regions, respectively, and $\sigma_{\text{background}}$ is the local background noise standard deviation. Binarisation density is monitored using the ink coverage check defined in Eqn. \ref{eqn:coverage}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eqn:coverage}
\text{Coverage} = \frac{\sum M(x, y) == 255}{H \times W} \times 100\%
\end{equation}

To measure the effectiveness of illumination normalisation, colour channel statistical properties are evaluated. The mean ($\mu$), standard deviation ($\sigma$), skewness ($S$), and kurtosis ($K$) are calculated using the statistical definitions in Eqn. \ref{eqn:mean}, \ref{eqn:std_dev}, \ref{eqn:skewness}, and \ref{eqn:kurtosis}:
\begin{equation}
\label{eqn:mean}
\text{Mean } \mu = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N x_i
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eqn:std_dev}
\text{Std Dev } \sigma = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (x_i - \mu)^2}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eqn:skewness}
\text{Skewness } S = \frac{1}{N \cdot \sigma^3} \sum_{i=1}^N (x_i - \mu)^3
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eqn:kurtosis}
\text{Kurtosis } K = \left[ \frac{1}{N \cdot \sigma^4} \sum_{i=1}^N (x_i - \mu)^4 \right] - 3
\end{equation}

	\hline

	\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Several key operational inferences are drawn from the experimental data:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Adaptive Local Sauvola Thresholding:} While Otsu global binarisation performs well on paper manuscripts under uniform illumination ($SSIM \ge 0.95$), it fails on weathered stone and copper plates due to local staining, producing large solid black components. The local Sauvola thresholding handles lighting variances effectively.
	
	\item \textbf{Character-Level Local Segmentations:} On palm-leaf manuscripts, organic decay along the leaf margins creates false components that intersect text lines. Running character-level cropping and local thresholding ensures margining noise is excluded, preserving faded glyph strokes.
	
	\item \textbf{Deep Learning Fallbacks:} The LightUNet and DocEnTr neural binarisation networks provide clean strokes on standard scripts. However, when processing highly deteriorated patinas or unfamiliar characters, the output entropy increases. Setting an entropy fallback threshold of $< 0.65$ triggers the robust, mathematical Sauvola routing, ensuring stable pipeline output.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Summary}

This chapter presented the experimental results, visual comparisons, and performance evaluation metrics of the digitisation pipeline. The simulation results illustrated the step-wise visual output for stone, paper, and copper plate inputs, demonstrating the impact of CLAHE, NLM, and Sauvola stages. Quantitative self-reference metrics and channel stats verified that the pipeline satisfies ECE contrast goals and corrects exposure imbalances. Lastly, throughput latency and cloud-to-local benchmarks highlighted execution performance. In Chapter 6, these findings are consolidated into final design conclusions, summaries of tasks performed, and suggestions for future script transcription extensions.
