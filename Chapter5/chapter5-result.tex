\chapter{Results and Discussions}

The Phase~1 digitisation pipeline is evaluated against the quality targets established in Chapter~3 using a set of representative test images drawn from all five artefact categories. This chapter presents the evaluation dataset composition, the quantitative image quality metric results (PSNR, SSIM, CNR, and edge sharpness), the throughput profiling that identifies the pipeline bottleneck, a qualitative analysis of the before/after enhancement outcomes, and a consolidated discussion of the inferences drawn from these results. Together, these evaluations confirm the pipeline's readiness as a Phase~1 foundation for scalable heritage image processing.

\section{Image Quality Metrics}

\subsection{Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio}

PSNR measures the fidelity of the enhanced image relative to the original scan, expressed in decibels. Higher PSNR indicates that the enhancement suppresses noise without introducing large per-pixel deviations from the original content. The design target is PSNR $\geq 30$ dB. Table~\ref{tab:psnr_results} reports mean PSNR and standard deviation across all images in each artefact category.

All five artefact categories exceed the 30\,dB target. Paper manuscripts score highest (34.8\,dB) because their relatively uniform backgrounds provide a clean baseline for denoising with minimal per-pixel deviation. Cave paintings score lowest (30.2\,dB) due to the high spatial variability of rock surfaces, but still clear the threshold. The overall mean of 32.3\,dB confirms that the pipeline's enhancement does not introduce substantial distortion while improving image quality.

\subsection{Contrast-to-Noise Ratio and Edge Sharpness}

Edge sharpness approximately doubles across all categories, confirming that the Real-ESRGAN super-resolution and unsharp masking recover character stroke definition without blurring.

Real-ESRGAN super-resolution (Stage~2) is the Phase~1 pipeline bottleneck, consuming 71\% of total processing time per image. The current configuration processes approximately 21 images per minute on a single GPU, comfortably exceeding the Phase~1 throughput target of 10 images per minute. For institutional-scale processing, the IEM team's workflow analysis identifies two scalability levers: batch GPU inference (processing multiple images per forward pass) and multi-GPU deployment to bring Stage~2 time below 1\,s per image, enabling a throughput increase to over 60 images per minute.

Text that is wholly invisible in the original photograph becomes clearly discernible after the colour decorrelation step. \textbf{Copper plates:} HDR normalisation suppresses specular highlights from the reflective copper surface, making the engraved Sanskrit script legible where the original photograph showed only bright reflective glare. However, residual highlights on the most severely oxidised samples still produce some binarisation errors, confirming that copper plate processing will benefit from LaMa inpainting in Phase~2.

\textbf{Paper manuscripts:} Foxing spots and staining are visually suppressed through denoising and contrast adjustment, and ink strokes appear crisper in the enhanced image. Paper manuscripts produce the cleanest binary outputs across all categories, reflecting their superior baseline image quality.

\textbf{Cave paintings:} DStretch in YBK colour space separates ochre pigment from the stone substrate in images where the original scan shows only faint, barely distinguishable tonal differences. The enhanced images reveal figurative outlines and geometric patterns that are not visible in the raw scan without specialist viewing.

\section{Discussion and Inferences}

The quantitative and qualitative results support several key inferences about the pipeline's design and Phase~1 performance.

\textbf{Artefact type is the primary performance driver:} Paper manuscripts consistently outperform stone inscriptions on every image quality metric, reflecting the inherent difference in scan quality and substrate legibility between the two media. This pattern suggests that Phase~2 effort should specifically target stone inscription enhancement quality, as this category presents the steepest degradation challenge alongside its historical significance.

\textbf{SSIM is a reliable proxy for enhancement quality:} Across all artefact categories, SSIM values above 0.87 correspond to the highest CNR and sharpness gains. This correlation suggests that SSIM can be used as a fast pre-screening indicator to identify images where enhancement has been most effective, enabling automated quality-based prioritisation before downstream Phase~2 processing begins.

\textbf{Copper plates require extended processing:} Residual specular highlights in the binarised copper plate output indicate that HDR normalisation alone does not fully suppress metallic reflectance artefacts. LaMa inpainting, planned for Phase~2, will address metallic surface masking as a pre-binarisation step for this category.

\textbf{Real-ESRGAN is the pipeline bottleneck:} At 2.0\,s per image (71\% of total processing time), Stage~2 GPU inference is the clear scalability constraint. Batch GPU processing and multi-GPU deployment are the primary Phase~2 infrastructure priorities for institutional-scale throughput.

The results demonstrate that the Phase~1 pipeline meets its design targets across all five artefact categories. The image quality metrics, throughput, and qualitative before/after analysis collectively confirm a functional, validated Phase~1 system suitable for institutional deployment and Phase~2 extension. Chapter~6 synthesises these findings into project-level conclusions and identifies the priorities for future development.
