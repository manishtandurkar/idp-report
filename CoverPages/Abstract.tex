Historical inscriptions and manuscripts across South Asia preserve invaluable linguistic, cultural, and archaeological information, yet many of these artefacts remain difficult to study because the available scans are degraded by noise, uneven illumination, fading ink, surface weathering, and background texture. These degradation modes reduce visual legibility and make the scans unsuitable for direct analysis without prior enhancement. This report addresses that gap by presenting a non-destructive, multi-artefact image processing pipeline that improves the visual quality of scanned historical artefacts and produces clean binary images ready for downstream processing, without altering the original sources.

The work covers three sequential stages: preprocessing (CLAHE brightness normalisation, grey-world white balance correction, EXIF orientation handling, and border removal), AI-based enhancement (Real-ESRGAN super-resolution, non-local means denoising, DStretch colour decorrelation, and unsharp masking through artefact-specific processing chains), and adaptive binarisation (Sauvola local thresholding with morphological closing and noise blob removal). Five categories of historical artefacts are supported — stone inscriptions, palm leaf manuscripts, copper plate inscriptions, paper manuscripts, and cave and rock paintings — each routed through a tailored enhancement chain matched to its dominant degradation characteristics.

The implementation uses a software stack built around OpenCV, Pillow, Real-ESRGAN, PyTorch, and scikit-image. A set of 38 representative test images spanning all five artefact categories is used to evaluate the pipeline against four objective quality metrics: Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio, Structural Similarity Index, Contrast-to-Noise Ratio, and edge sharpness. The results show that the pipeline consistently meets its quality targets across all artefact types, with a mean PSNR of 32.3\,dB and mean SSIM of 0.88, establishing a validated image quality foundation on which downstream processing stages can be built in Phase~2.
