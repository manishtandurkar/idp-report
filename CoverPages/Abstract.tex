Historical inscriptions and manuscripts across South Asia preserve invaluable linguistic, cultural, and archaeological information, yet many of these artefacts remain difficult to study because the available scans are degraded by noise, uneven illumination, fading ink, surface weathering, and background texture. These limitations reduce readability and make manual transcription slow, error-prone, and dependent on rare specialist expertise. This report addresses that gap by presenting a non-destructive digitisation pipeline that improves the visual quality of scanned artefacts and prepares them for searchable transcription without altering the original sources.

The work focuses on a staged workflow that begins with preprocessing and continues through AI-based enhancement and binarisation to produce clean character regions. The pipeline combines image normalisation, denoising, contrast improvement, super-resolution, and adaptive local thresholding to make degraded text more legible, preparing character edges for downstream recognition. Additionally, a script-aware ensemble OCR routing framework is architecturally designed, alongside a structured record assembly schema to consolidate the processed assets and metadata in a preservation-ready format.

The implementation and evaluation use a software stack built around OpenCV, Pillow, Real-ESRGAN, FastAPI, and a React-based web interface, with the OCR routing planned around Tesseract and EasyOCR. Representative inscription and manuscript images from different material types, including stone, palm leaf, copper plate, paper, and cave or rock art, are used as test cases to verify the pipeline’s robustness across varied degradation patterns. The results show that the proposed workflow significantly improves contrast, suppresses noise, and produces clearer character regions, making it a practical foundation for scalable digital preservation and future extension to automated transcription, translation, and metadata extraction.
