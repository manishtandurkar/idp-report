\chapter{Conclusion and Future Scope}

The Inscription and Manuscript Digitisation Project addressed a well-documented access gap in South Asian heritage scholarship: the absence of an automated, multi-artefact pipeline that transforms degraded archival scans into searchable, citable digital records. This chapter synthesises the outcomes against the three stated project objectives, acknowledges the limitations encountered in Phase 1, and outlines the planned extensions for Phase 2 and beyond. A set of learning outcomes drawn from all three disciplinary streams closes the chapter.

\section{Conclusion}

South Asia preserves millions of historical inscriptions and manuscripts whose physical condition and geographic dispersal have limited their accessibility to researchers, historians, linguists, and the broader public. While major archives provide scanned images, they rarely provide a reliable transcription layer that enables text to be searched, compared, or cited in structured form. This project addressed three specific objectives to close that gap: first, to build a non-destructive, five-artefact digitisation pipeline covering stone inscriptions, palm leaf manuscripts, copper plates, paper manuscripts, and cave paintings, with preprocessing and AI enhancement stages that improve legibility; second, to extract Unicode transcriptions through adaptive binarisation and a dual-engine OCR ensemble with confidence-based output tagging; and third, to generate structured, citable digital records exportable as JSON and researcher-ready PDFs. These objectives form a complete Phase 1 pipeline from a raw degraded scan to a preserved archival digital record.

All three objectives are fully achieved in Phase 1. The preprocessing stage normalises raw scans through CLAHE brightness correction, grey-world white balance, EXIF orientation handling, and border removal, producing a consistent quality baseline. The enhancement stage applies Real-ESRGAN super-resolution, non-local means denoising, DStretch colour decorrelation, and unsharp masking through five artefact-specific processing chains, each tailored to the dominant degradation mode of its material type. The binarisation stage uses Sauvola adaptive thresholding as the primary method, with morphological closing and noise blob removal refining the binary output for OCR. The OCR stage runs Tesseract and EasyOCR in parallel and merges their outputs by maximum confidence, supporting Tamil, Sanskrit, Kannada, Telugu, Malayalam, and Devanagari scripts. The record assembly stage produces structured JSON records with full artefact metadata and generates multi-page researcher-ready PDFs via \texttt{fpdf2}. The React and FastAPI web interface delivers these capabilities through an accessible browser-based portal with before/after comparison and asynchronous job processing.

The evaluation across 38 test images spanning all five artefact categories confirms quantitative compliance with all primary design targets. The mean PSNR of 32.3\,dB and SSIM of 0.88 exceed the 30\,dB and 0.85 thresholds. The ensemble OCR configuration achieves a mean confidence of 0.81 and a character error rate of 0.12 across the four textual categories, with the dual-engine design reducing CER by 33\% over either single engine in isolation. The pipeline processes approximately 10 images per minute on a single GPU, meeting the throughput target. Qualitative before/after evaluation confirms that DStretch reveals inscription text wholly invisible in the original stone scan, CLAHE corrects shadow-induced contrast loss in palm leaf photographs, and Real-ESRGAN recovers diacritical mark detail that field photography blurred. The only category marginally outside its CER target is copper plates (0.17 vs. 0.15), attributed to residual specular highlight artefacts that the current HDR normalisation does not fully suppress. The project thus delivers a validated Phase 1 system that transforms degraded historical scans into machine-readable, citable digital records and establishes a solid foundation for Phase 2 extension.

\section{Future Scope}

Phase 1 identifies several limitations that define the priority directions for Phase 2 and longer-term development.

\textbf{Translation layer (Phase 2):} The translation stage is fully designed with model selection complete, but deferred pending robust OCR validation. The planned implementation will use Helsinki-NLP OPUS-MT models (\texttt{opus-mt-dra-en} for Dravidian scripts and \texttt{opus-mt-hi-en} for Hindi/Sanskrit) for texts dated after the 10th century CE, and an LLM fallback with artefact context provided as a system prompt for archaic classical forms where standard MT models degrade. The JSON record schema requires no structural change to accommodate this extension; the translation field will simply be populated from its current \texttt{null} state.

\textbf{Brahmi and Grantha OCR:} No production-ready off-the-shelf OCR model exists for these two scripts, which appear on a significant proportion of South Asian stone inscriptions and copper plates. A custom convolutional classifier trained on the 1032-character Ashokan Brahmi dataset \cite{Aitha2023} (258 character classes) is planned as a Phase 2 deliverable. Until this is available, artefacts in these scripts remain flagged for manual transcription.

\textbf{LaMa inpainting for heavy damage:} Images with more than 50\% surface damage (severe erosion, missing substrate, or deep staining) produce low-confidence OCR that manual review cannot reliably correct. Large Mask Inpainting (LaMa) will be integrated as an optional pre-binarisation step to fill missing surface regions before thresholding, targeting the heavily oxidised copper plate and severely eroded stone inscription categories first.

\textbf{3D inscription processing:} Deep-carved stone inscriptions benefit substantially from LiDAR or photogrammetry-derived depth information. The Kaggle Tamil Stone Inscriptions dataset includes 3D models alongside photographs. Processing 3D point clouds to generate surface normal maps and depth-enhanced contrast images is planned as a Phase 3 investigation.

\textbf{Multi-script region segmentation:} Several historical artefacts carry inscriptions in two scripts on a single surface (for example, Tamil body text with Grantha Sanskrit annotations). The current pipeline assumes a single script per submitted image. A future segmentation module will divide the image into script-homogeneous regions before routing each to the appropriate OCR configuration.

\textbf{Public-facing research portal:} The current React and FastAPI interface is a research and development tool. Phase 7 (long-term) will deploy a public-facing portal connected to an institutional archive, providing open access to all digitised records for academic researchers, students, and the general public under a CC BY 4.0 licence.

\section{Learning Outcomes of the Project}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Acquired hands-on expertise in AI-driven image enhancement, specifically in deploying Real-ESRGAN super-resolution and DStretch colour decorrelation for culturally significant heritage imagery, developing a practical understanding of how deep learning models can be applied to non-standard domains beyond their training distribution.
    \item Gained experience designing and evaluating ensemble machine learning systems, including the implementation of a dual-engine OCR pipeline combining Tesseract and EasyOCR with confidence-based result merging, and developing a quantitative understanding of how complementary failure modes can be exploited to improve overall system performance.
    \item Developed full-stack software engineering competencies through the design and implementation of a FastAPI REST backend, a React 19 and Vite 6 single-page application, asynchronous job management with TanStack Query, and a continuous integration pipeline using GitHub Actions.
    \item Applied signal processing theory in a practical engineering context through the ECE team's contributions: characterising noise profiles across five artefact categories, designing a Gabor filter bank for inscription texture separation, implementing FFT-based periodic noise removal, and computing objective image quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, CNR, Laplacian variance sharpness) as validation tools.
    \item Developed an understanding of archival preservation principles, including non-destructive processing policies, the 3-2-1 backup rule, EXIF metadata management, processing log standards, and the production of CC BY 4.0 licensed citable digital records, appreciating how technology must align with cultural and ethical preservation responsibilities.
    \item Gained practical project management skills through the IEM team's contributions: constructing a risk register covering schedule, data, technical, and quality risks; conducting value stream mapping to identify the OCR bottleneck and propose mitigation strategies; performing a cost-benefit analysis comparing automated and manual transcription at scale; and producing stakeholder documentation for museum curators and heritage institutions.
    \item Practised collaborative interdisciplinary software development across three engineering disciplines, including cross-team code review, Agile sprint planning with asynchronous standups, integration testing of components with different disciplinary ownership, and the experience of converging three distinct technical perspectives into a single coherent system design.
\end{itemize}
