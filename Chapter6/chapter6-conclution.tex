\chapter{Conclusion and Future Scope}

The Inscription and Manuscript Digitisation Project addressed a fundamental image-quality barrier in South Asian heritage scholarship: the absence of an automated, multi-artefact pipeline that transforms degraded archival scans into visually legible, clean binary images ready for downstream transcription. This chapter synthesises the outcomes against the three stated project objectives, acknowledges the limitations encountered in Phase~1, and outlines the planned extensions for Phase~2 and beyond. A set of learning outcomes drawn from all three disciplinary streams closes the chapter.

\section{Conclusion}

South Asia preserves millions of historical inscriptions and manuscripts whose physical condition and geographic dispersal have limited their accessibility to researchers, historians, linguists, and the broader public. While major archives provide scanned images, the visual quality of those images is frequently insufficient for legible reading or reliable downstream processing: noise, uneven illumination, fading, and surface weathering render large portions of each scan illegible. This project addressed three specific objectives to close that gap: first, to build a non-destructive, five-artefact preprocessing and AI enhancement pipeline covering stone inscriptions, palm leaf manuscripts, copper plates, paper manuscripts, and cave paintings, with artefact-specific chains that improve image legibility; second, to produce high-quality binary images through adaptive Sauvola binarisation with morphological refinement, providing clean text-background separation as a foundation for downstream Phase~2 processing; and third, to validate the quality of enhanced and binarised outputs using objective image quality metrics --- PSNR, SSIM, CNR, and edge sharpness --- across all five artefact categories.

All three objectives are fully achieved in Phase~1. The preprocessing stage normalises raw scans through CLAHE brightness correction, grey-world white balance, EXIF orientation handling, and border removal, producing a consistent quality baseline. The enhancement stage applies Real-ESRGAN super-resolution, non-local means denoising, DStretch colour decorrelation, and unsharp masking through five artefact-specific processing chains, each tailored to the dominant degradation mode of its material type. The binarisation stage uses Sauvola adaptive thresholding as the primary method, with morphological closing and noise blob removal refining the binary output. The ECE team's signal processing modules --- a 24-filter Gabor filter bank, FFT-based periodic noise removal, and colour channel analysis --- integrate into the pipeline and inform DStretch parameter selection and binarisation refinement. The IEM team's workflow analysis identifies Real-ESRGAN as the pipeline bottleneck and proposes batch GPU processing as the primary Phase~2 scalability lever.

The evaluation across 38 test images spanning all five artefact categories confirms quantitative compliance with all primary design targets. The mean PSNR of 32.3\,dB and SSIM of 0.88 exceed the 30\,dB and 0.85 thresholds. CNR improvements range from $2.1\times$ for stone inscriptions to $1.9\times$ for palm leaf manuscripts, and edge sharpness approximately doubles across all categories, confirming that the Real-ESRGAN super-resolution and unsharp masking recover character detail without blurring. The pipeline processes approximately 21 images per minute on a single GPU, exceeding the Phase~1 throughput target of 10 images per minute. Qualitative before/after evaluation confirms that DStretch reveals inscription features wholly invisible in the original stone scan, CLAHE corrects shadow-induced contrast loss in palm leaf photographs, and Real-ESRGAN recovers fine character detail that field photography blurred. The only category with a residual processing challenge is copper plates, where specular highlight artefacts partially persist in the binary output and will be addressed by LaMa inpainting in Phase~2. The project thus delivers a validated Phase~1 system that transforms degraded historical scans into enhanced, legible images and clean binary outputs, establishing a solid foundation for Phase~2 processing.

\section{Future Scope}

Phase~1 identifies several limitations that define the priority directions for Phase~2 and longer-term development.

\textbf{Character Recognition and Transcription (Phase 2):} The primary Phase~2 deliverable is an automated character recognition stage supporting Tamil, Sanskrit, Kannada, Telugu, Malayalam, and Devanagari scripts, with confidence-based output tagging. A custom model for Brahmi script using the 1032-character Ashokan Brahmi dataset \cite{Aitha2023} is also planned.

\textbf{Translation (Phase 3):} Once Phase~2 character recognition is validated, translation of transcribed text into modern English is planned as a Phase~3 extension.

\textbf{LaMa inpainting for heavy damage:} Images with more than 50\% surface damage (severe erosion, missing substrate, or deep staining) produce degraded binary output that manual review cannot reliably correct. Large Mask Inpainting (LaMa) will be integrated as an optional pre-binarisation step to fill missing surface regions, targeting the heavily oxidised copper plate and severely eroded stone inscription categories first.

\textbf{3D inscription processing:} Deep-carved stone inscriptions benefit substantially from LiDAR or photogrammetry-derived depth information. The Kaggle Tamil Stone Inscriptions dataset includes 3D models alongside photographs. Processing 3D point clouds to generate surface normal maps and depth-enhanced contrast images is planned as a Phase~3 investigation.

\textbf{Multi-script region segmentation:} Several historical artefacts carry inscriptions in two scripts on a single surface (for example, Tamil body text with Grantha Sanskrit annotations). A future segmentation module will divide the image into script-homogeneous regions before routing each to the appropriate Phase~2 processing configuration.

\textbf{Public-facing research portal:} A long-term Phase~5 goal is a public-facing portal connected to an institutional archive, providing open access to all digitised records for academic researchers, students, and the general public under a CC BY 4.0 licence.

\section{Learning Outcomes of the Project}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Acquired hands-on expertise in AI-driven image enhancement, specifically in deploying Real-ESRGAN super-resolution and DStretch colour decorrelation for culturally significant heritage imagery, developing a practical understanding of how deep learning models can be applied to non-standard domains beyond their training distribution.
    \item Applied signal processing theory in a practical engineering context through the ECE team's contributions: characterising noise profiles across five artefact categories, designing a Gabor filter bank for inscription texture separation, implementing FFT-based periodic noise removal, and computing objective image quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, CNR, Laplacian variance sharpness) as pipeline validation tools.
    \item Developed an understanding of archival preservation principles, including non-destructive processing policies, the 3-2-1 backup rule, EXIF metadata management, processing log standards, and the requirements for producing citable, reproducible digital records, appreciating how technology must align with cultural and ethical preservation responsibilities.
    \item Gained practical project management skills through the IEM team's contributions: constructing a risk register covering schedule, data, technical, and quality risks; conducting value stream mapping to identify the Real-ESRGAN bottleneck and propose batch GPU mitigation strategies; performing a cost-benefit analysis comparing automated and manual image preparation at scale; and producing stakeholder documentation for museum curators and heritage institutions.
    \item Practised collaborative interdisciplinary software development across three engineering disciplines, including cross-team code review, Agile sprint planning with asynchronous standups, integration testing of components with different disciplinary ownership, and the experience of converging three distinct technical perspectives into a single coherent pipeline design.
\end{itemize}
